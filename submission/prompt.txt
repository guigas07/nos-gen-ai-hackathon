MASKING RULE (apply mechanically and exactly)
- A "word" is any maximal run of non-whitespace characters.
- Every word has a "core" (its letters/digits/internal punctuation, e.g.
  internal hyphens, slashes, dots, @ signs) and may have leading/trailing
  sentence punctuation attached (commas, periods, colons, parentheses,
  quotes).
  - This applies even to longer multi-word phrases: a street name such as
  "Rua das Flores" (three words) must become three separate asterisks,
  "* * *" - never a single "*" for the whole phrase. Always count the
  words in the original and output exactly that many asterisks, one per
  word, never fewer.
- To mask a word: replace its core with exactly one asterisk character
  "*", but KEEP any leading/trailing sentence punctuation exactly where
  it was. Examples: "Ana" -> "*", "Correia" -> "*", "Maria," -> "*,",
  "(Carlos," -> "(*,", "ID-2024-0098" -> "*" (internal hyphens are part
  of the identifier, not sentence punctuation, so the whole token
  becomes one asterisk).
- Never merge two words into a single asterisk, and never split one word
  into multiple asterisks.
- Every word that is NOT identifying information must be copied into the
  output completely unchanged - same spelling, accents, capitalization
  and punctuation as in the input. This is required to preserve the
  document's original formatting.
- Do not add, remove, reorder, translate or summarize any words besides
  this masking.
- Keep every line break exactly where it is in the input, except: delete
  every empty line. Do not insert new line breaks.
- Output ONLY the resulting document text. No commentary, no headers, no
  explanations, no code fences, no notes.

WHAT COUNTS AS IDENTIFYING (mask it)

Direct identifiers - things that name or number a specific person:
- Full names of any person mentioned, including first-name-only mentions
  once a person has been introduced, and nicknames or initials used to
  refer to them.
- Government or institutional ID numbers tied to a person (national ID,
  tax number, social security number, passport, student/employee/
  patient/client number, professional license number).
- Contact details: phone numbers, email addresses, home or personal
  addresses, usernames or handles.
- Financial account identifiers: bank account/IBAN, card numbers, CVV,
  expiry dates, insurance or policy numbers.
- Biometric or device identifiers: fingerprint ID, facial recognition
  ID, device serial numbers tied to a person.
- Dates of birth.

Indirect identifiers - things that, alone or combined with other details
in the document, could single out one specific real person even without
naming them:
- An exact age, date, or duration stated specifically about a named or
  otherwise identifiable person (not a generic statistic).
- A role/title/employer/institution combination specific enough, in
  context, to point to one person.
- A rare or unusual personal attribute (a specific condition, habit, or
  characteristic) when combined with other details in the document in a
  way that narrows identity down to one plausible individual.
- Any other detail that functions, in this document, as a de facto
  identifier for one specific person, even though it is not a name or a
  number.

WHAT IS NOT IDENTIFYING (keep unchanged)
- The document's own metadata when it describes the document, record or
  institution itself rather than a person - e.g. a document title, a
  filing or report date, a case or tracking reference number used to
  file the document - UNLESS that date or number is itself a personal
  identifier of an individual (like a date of birth or a personal
  account number).
- Section headers, field labels and other structural text (e.g. "Name:",
  "Date:") - these are not personal data themselves, only the values
  that follow them can be.
- Generic, non-identifying narrative content: general descriptions,
  categories, terminology, or statements that remain true of many people
  and don't, by themselves or in combination, narrow identity down to
  one person.

If you are unsure whether something identifies a specific individual,
prefer to mask it when it is a proper name, an ID/account number, a
contact detail, or a detail that - combined with the rest of the
document - would let a reader single out one real person. Do not mask
generic, non-identifying vocabulary.
